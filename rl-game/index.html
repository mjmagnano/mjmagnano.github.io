Get out the vote

You are a canvasser on election day. You have 25 moves before the polls close. Two of the three houses are your supporters: knock on their doors and they go vote. The third house is not a supporter. Can you turn out both supporters, and then can a computer learn to do it by trial and error?

1. Play the game

PlayingYou Moves left25 Supporters voted0 of 2 Score0

Use the arrow keys or buttons. Walk onto a house to knock on its door.

The state is the canvasser's square (25 options) plus which supporters have already voted (4 options), so the game has 25 × 4 = 100 states. The clock is not part of the state: the agent never sees how many moves are left.

2. Rewards

Each move earns points from the reward() function. Change the numbers to change what the agent is trying to do. New numbers count from the next move; reset the agent to retrain it from scratch.

3. A Q-learning agent

The agent keeps a table with one number for each of the 100 states × 4 moves: its guess of how many points that move leads to. All guesses start at 0. After every move it nudges the guess toward points just earned + 0.95 × best guess from the new square.

Exploration ε = 0.2 (1 move in 5 is random) · learning rate 0.2 · discount 0.95 · ties broken at random.
